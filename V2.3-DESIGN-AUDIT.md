# Existing v2.2 design audit and upgrade

The starting ZIP was inspected before modification. It contained the seven separate React chapter implementations, a local deterministic judgment adapter, versioned Player Vault storage with migration/restore, role declarations and team habit analysis, a server-only Gemini adapter, optional consent-gated writing/title generation and supported music thumbnail metadata.

The existing landing page used a supplied bitmap, yellow/charcoal palette and Impact display typography. Chapter 1 hero selection used small initials tiles. Chapter screens reused dark rectangular panels, short introductions and role symbols. Chapter 7 used a text-only archetype roster and a reveal panel. The vault was a dense sequence of forms and plain sections. AI writing help and title editing already existed; replacing them would have discarded useful consent and persistence work.

The supplied bitmap had no accompanying license or attribution record. The upgrade preserves the file while making original role illustrations the default. Media configuration records local paths, alt text, credit and permission basis. Missing, invalid or failed portraits use original role mascots. This gives a usable visual baseline while allowing properly authorized hero artwork later.

The upgrade adds illustrated selection cards, seven narrative banners, illustrated archetype cards, clearer mobile layout, larger touch targets, visible keyboard focus and a reduced-motion-safe reveal. It keeps comic display typography for titles while preserving ordinary readable body text. A shared narrative studio adds consent-based chapter, individual and team writing, editable previews, explicit acceptance, local alternatives, evidence provenance and portable text-only export/import.

The existing deterministic scoring and chapter schemas are unchanged. Creative titles remain distinct from scoring identities. Stable local labels vary using the included evidence fingerprint and clearly identify their creative basis. AI naming is asked to use evidence-specific motivations and tensions rather than a fixed name roster. The default role mascots are intentionally generic role illustrations; character-specific licensed portraits are not bundled.
